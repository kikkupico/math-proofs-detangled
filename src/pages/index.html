The idea

Every proof answers five questions

Scroll, and watch one small proof light up as each question is answered. The five parts of this guide follow the same order.

1 · Purpose

Why can't we just check?

2 + 4 = 6. 10 + 8 = 18. −6 + 12 = 6. Every pair you try works. But there are infinitely many pairs, and checking a million of them still leaves infinitely many unchecked. A proof covers them all at once.

2 · Tools

What exactly is being claimed?

English hides structure. Rewritten with quantifiers and connectives, the claim has a visible shape: for all m and n, if both are even, then m + n is even. The shape tells you how to prove it.

3 · Techniques

Which move comes next?

A “for all” goal means: let the objects be arbitrary. An “if–then” goal means: assume the if-part. A definition (even) gets unpacked. Each move is standard, and each one writes a line.

4 · Examples

Put it together.

The finished proof is just the moves, in order, written as sentences. Part 4 does exactly this for bigger theorems: infinitely many primes, the irrationality of √2, Cantor's theorem.

5 · Craft

Would a reader be convinced?

A correct argument still has to be readable: every letter introduced, every step given a reason. Part 5 is about that craft: reading proofs, writing them, catching common mistakes, and what to do when you're stuck.

Claim

The sum of any two even integers is even.

∀m ∀n ((m even ∧ n even) → m + n even)
Proof
Let m and n be arbitrary integers. Suppose m and n are both even. Then m = 2j and n = 2k for some integers j, k. So m + n = 2j + 2k = 2(j + k), which is even. Since m and n were arbitrary, the claim holds. ∎
