Read the shape. Make the move.

At every point in a proof you have some things you know (the givens) and one thing you want (the goal). The logical shape of the goal, or of a given, tells you what to do next. Each page here is one move, shown on a small example.

Givens

  • n is an integer
  • n is even

Goal

  • n even → n² even
  • n² is even

A goal of the form P → Q became a new given P and a new goal Q. That's one move.

Ten techniques

Direct proof

To prove P → Q, assume P and derive Q.

P ⇒ Q 2

Contrapositive

Prove ¬Q → ¬P instead. Same statement, easier start.

¬Q ⇒ ¬P 3

Contradiction

Assume the goal is false and derive something impossible.

⊥ 4

Arbitrary objects

To prove ∀x P(x), let x be arbitrary and prove P(x).

∀ 5

Witnesses

To prove ∃x P(x), find a value that works.

∃ 6

And & iff

Split ∧ and ↔ goals into separate halves.

∧ ↔ 7

Cases

Use P ∨ Q by proving the goal in each case.

∨ 8

Uniqueness

Exactly one: show one exists, and any two are equal.

∃! 9

Counterexamples

Disprove a “for all” with a single failure.

¬∀ 10

Induction

Prove ∀n ∈ ℕ P(n) with a base case and a step.

n → n+1

“I shall make no assertion before its time.”

Velleman's motto for proof writing. Every line must be justified by what came before it. You can assume things temporarily, as long as you know that's what you're doing.
