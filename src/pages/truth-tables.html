A formula with two letters has only four possible situations. List them all, work out the formula in each, and you know everything about it. That's a truth table, and it settles questions of validity and equivalence with no cleverness at all.

The three basic connectives
How it works

Inside out, one column at a time

To find the truth table of ¬(P ∧ Q), list every combination of P and Q. Then work from the inside: first P ∧ Q, then negate that column.

With n letters there are 2ⁿ rows: 4 for two letters, 8 for three. Tedious for big formulas, but completely mechanical.

List all four combinations of truth values for P and Q.

Work out the inner part, P ∧ Q. It's true only in the first row.

Negate that column to get ¬(P ∧ Q). It's false only in the first row.

The lab

Are these the same?

Two formulas are equivalent (written ≡) when their columns agree in every row. Then you can swap one for the other anywhere, which is how proofs simplify statements.

Worth remembering

The laws you'll use most

Each one is a pair with matching truth tables. Check any of them in the lab above.

De Morgan's laws¬(P ∧ Q) ≡ ¬P ∨ ¬Q
¬(P ∨ Q) ≡ ¬P ∧ ¬Q

Pushing a ¬ inside flips ∧ and ∨. Used in almost every proof that involves a negation.

Distributive lawsP ∧ (Q ∨ R) ≡ (P ∧ Q) ∨ (P ∧ R)

And the same with ∧ and ∨ swapped. Like multiplying out brackets.

Double negation¬¬P ≡ P

Two negations cancel.

AbsorptionP ∨ (P ∧ Q) ≡ P

Also P ∧ (P ∨ Q) ≡ P.

Commutative, associative, idempotent

Order and grouping don't matter for ∧ or ∨, and P ∧ P ≡ P, P ∨ P ≡ P.

Tautologies and contradictions

P ∨ ¬P is true in every row: a tautology. P ∧ ¬P is false in every row: a contradiction. Proof by contradiction aims for the second kind.

Checking arguments

Validity, by brute force

The butler argument from Part 1 had premises B ∨ M and M ∨ C, and conclusion B ∨ C. An argument is valid if every row where all premises are true also has the conclusion true.

Scan the table for a row where both premises are T but the conclusion is F. Row 6 (B false, M true, C false) is that row: only the maid did it. So the argument is invalid.
