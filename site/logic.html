Sentential Logic

The grammar of certainty.

Before you can prove a statement, you need to see its shape. This chapter gives you five connectives, truth tables to check them, and sets, which turn out to be logic in disguise.

Key ideas

Validity

The premises can't all be true while the conclusion is false.

That's what makes an argument valid. Whether the premises really are true is a separate question.

∴
Form over content

Validity depends only on the shape.

Replace the sentences with letters P, Q, R and you can judge an argument without knowing what it's about.

P ∨ Q
Truth tables

Check any formula by brute force.

List every combination of true and false for the letters and work out the formula row by row. Two formulas are equivalent when their columns match.

De Morgan's laws

Negation flips ∧ and ∨.

“Not (P and Q)” means “not P, or not Q”. You'll use this in almost every proof.

¬(P ∧ Q) ≡ ¬P ∨ ¬Q
The conditional

P → Q is false in exactly one case.

P true and Q false. In every other case it counts as true, including whenever P is false.

P → Q
Sets

Set operations are connectives in disguise.

x ∈ A ∩ B means x ∈ A ∧ x ∈ B. Union is ∨, and difference is “and not”.

∩ ∪ ∖

The laws you'll lean on

Each one is a pair of formulas with matching truth tables. The book uses them to simplify statements before proving them.

De Morgan's laws
¬(P ∧ Q) ≡ ¬P ∨ ¬Q
¬(P ∨ Q) ≡ ¬P ∧ ¬Q

When you push a negation inside, ∧ and ∨ swap.

Conditional laws
P → Q ≡ ¬P ∨ Q
P → Q ≡ ¬(P ∧ ¬Q)

An if–then can be rewritten with ∨ or ∧, and the reverse.

Contrapositive
P → Q ≡ ¬Q → ¬P

The basis of a whole proof strategy in Chapter 3.

Distributive
P ∧ (Q ∨ R) ≡ (P ∧ Q) ∨ (P ∧ R)

The same holds with ∧ and ∨ swapped.

Absorption
P ∨ (P ∧ Q) ≡ P

Also P ∧ (P ∨ Q) ≡ P.

Tautologies & contradictions

Always true. Always false.

P ∨ ¬P is true in every row (a tautology). P ∧ ¬P is false in every row (a contradiction). Proof by contradiction aims at a statement of the second kind.

Also in the toolkit

Commutative, associative, idempotent, double negation.

Order and grouping don't matter for ∧ or ∨. P ∧ P ≡ P. And ¬¬P ≡ P.

§1.3–1.4

Sets are logic you can draw

A set is a collection of objects. {x | P(x)} is the truth set of P: everything that makes P(x) true. In that notation x is a bound variable. A free variable is one whose value you have to supply before the statement can be true or false.

Each set operation is defined by a connective. That's why the laws of logic carry straight over to sets.
