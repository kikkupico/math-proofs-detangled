The Pigeonhole Principle

More pigeons than holes: two must share.

If n + 1 objects go into n boxes, some box gets at least two. It sounds too obvious to be useful. The skill is in choosing the boxes: once you've picked them well, the principle finishes the proof in one line.

General form

If N objects go into k boxes, some box holds at least ⌈N/k⌉.

⌈x⌉ means x rounded up. Why it's true, by contradiction: if every box held fewer than N/k, the total would be less than k · N/k = N.

Try to beat it

Spread them out as evenly as you can

Set the number of objects and boxes, then drop objects in. The fullest box can never hold fewer than ⌈N/k⌉.

Spread evenly is the best anyone can do, and it still hits the bound exactly.

Choosing the boxes

Six numbers from 1 to 10

Claim: however you choose 6 different numbers from {1, …, 10}, two of them add up to 11.

The boxes are the five pairs that add to 11: {1, 10}, {2, 9}, {3, 8}, {4, 7}, {5, 6}. Six numbers, five pairs: two numbers land in the same pair, and they add to 11.

Try to choose six numbers that dodge it.
