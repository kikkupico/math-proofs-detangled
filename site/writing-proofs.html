How to Write a Proof

Sentences, not symbol soup.

Scratch work is for finding the proof. The written proof is for a reader who wasn't there: it introduces every object, says what is being assumed, and gives a reason for every claim. Writing it well also catches mistakes the scratch work hid.

Let introduce an object Suppose assume something (for now) Then a consequence of what came before Since …, give the reason Therefore the goal ∎

Five words do most of the work. Each one tells the reader what kind of sentence comes next.

Before and after

From draft to proof, one fix at a time

Claim: the product of two odd integers is odd. Start from a typical first draft. Each step applies one rule. Watch step 3: the draft had a mistake that only shows up when it's written properly.

The draft. Correct-looking symbols, but a reader can't tell what m, n and k are, or what's assumed and what's claimed.

Introduce every object. Say what m and n are, and that we're assuming they're odd. Use words, not ⇒ arrows.

One new name per object. Writing out “for some integer k” for both shows the bug: the draft used the same k twice, which only covers m = n. They need different names.

Redo the work. With separate names, the algebra changes. The draft's calculation was for n², not mn.

Give the reason. “Odd” means 2 · (an integer) + 1, so say that 2jk + j + k is an integer. Now every line follows from the ones before it.

m, n odd Let m and n be odd integers. m = 2k + 1, n = 2k + 1 Then m = 2j + 1 and n = 2k + 1 for some integers j and k. mn = 4k² + 4k + 1 = 2(2k² + 2k) + 1 So mn = (2j + 1)(2k + 1) = 4jk + 2j + 2k + 1 = 2(2jk + j + k) + 1. ∴ odd ∎ Since 2jk + j + k is an integer, mn is odd. ∎
Rules of thumb

Six habits of readable proofs

1

Write sentences

A proof is prose with some symbols in it. “Then”, “so” and “since” carry the logic that arrows hide.

2

Introduce every letter

Before using x, say what it is: “Let x be arbitrary”, “Let x = 3”, or “there is an integer x with …”.

3

One name per object

Two numbers promised by two different ∃ givens get two different names, even if they play the same role.

4

Announce the plan

“We prove the contrapositive.” “Suppose, for contradiction, …” “Case 1: …” One phrase saves the reader from guessing the structure.

5

Go forwards

Start from what you know and end at the goal. Scratch work often runs backwards from the goal; the written proof reverses it.

6

Leave out the search

How you found the witness belongs in scratch work. The proof just says “Let δ = ε/2” and shows it works.
