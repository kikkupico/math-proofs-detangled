The big idea

Structured programming,
meet structured proving

Programs aren't written as flat lists of instructions. They're built by nesting a few standard blocks like if–else and while. Velleman's insight is that proofs work the same way. Each logical form has its own block, and you nest the blocks to build the proof.

Chapters 1 and 2 teach you to read the logical form of a statement. Chapter 3 gives you the block for each form. Chapters 4–7 put the blocks to work on relations, functions, induction and infinity.

How the ideas connect

Arrows show which chapter builds on which. Dashed lines mark the places where the book comes back to an earlier idea.

Chapter 5 defines functions as a kind of relation, so it needs Chapter 4. Chapter 7 measures infinite sets with the one-to-one, onto functions from Chapter 5, and its proofs use induction from Chapter 6.

Three ways to use this guide

Speedrun

Read top to bottom.

Each page opens with a few highlight cards, then explains the main ideas in plain language. Read them all and you've covered every theme in the book.

Play

Poke at the ideas.

Every chapter has a hands-on demo: truth tables, a proof you build step by step, falling dominoes, Cantor's diagonal. Change things and watch what happens.

Map

Dive into the book.

Every page ends with In the book: each section, its page number, and what you'll get from it. Starred exercises have solutions in Appendix 1, starting on p. 329.
